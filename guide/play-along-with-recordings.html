Play along with recordings

On this page
  • Attach a recording
  • Play it in the reader
  • Change the tempo and key
  • Record yourself in the reader

Each piece can have a recording attached: a reference track, a backing track or a take of your own. It plays in a small player above the score, and you can slow it down or move it to another key while you learn the part. Recordings and Tempo & Key are free.

Attach a recording

  1. Open the piece's details with Edit Details (in the reader's title menu or ⋯ menu, or by touching and holding the piece in your music list).
  2. Tap Add Recording and choose Record (record with the microphone), Import File (an audio file from the Files app) or Music Library (a song from the Music app that's downloaded to your device).
  3. Save the details.

Protected tracks from the Music app can't be imported; only songs you own can be added.

A piece holds one recording. To replace it, tap Update Recording; to delete it, tap Update Recording and choose Remove Recording.

If the piece has no duration yet, the recording's length fills it in, which helps set-list timing. See Add pages and timing to a set list.

You can also start from the reader: tap Play on a piece that has no recording, and the same three choices appear.

Play it in the reader

Tap Play in the toolbar. A player appears under the toolbar with play and pause, a button to skip back 10 seconds, and a bar you can drag to move through the track. Turning pages and opening other tools don't stop it.

  • Tap to shrink the player to a small pill with the time, so it covers less of the score. Tap the pill to bring the player back.
  • Tap to stop and close the player.

Playback continues when you lock the screen or switch apps, and you can pause from the Lock Screen or your headphones. Unlike the metronome, a recording pauses music playing in other apps. Leaving the piece stops it.

Change the tempo and key

  1. While the recording is loaded, tap Tempo & Key in the player.
  2. Under Tempo, drag the slider or tap − and + to go slower or faster. Tap a mark under the slider to jump to half speed, three-quarter speed, the original or the fastest setting.
  3. Under Key, tap a key in the row, or drag the row, to move the recording up or down.

Every change plays right away. The tempo goes from half speed to 125% without changing the pitch, and the key moves up to six semitones either way without changing the tempo. The recording file itself is never changed, and the app remembers your settings for that recording. Tap on a card to return it to the original. Once you've changed something, the player's button shows what you're hearing (for example, "72 BPM · D") so a slowed-down track isn't mistaken for the real thing.

BPM or percent

The app measures the recording's tempo from the audio. It shows the tempo in BPM only when it's sure: when the recording holds a steady tempo, or when you've set the original tempo yourself. A recording whose tempo changes (common in classical music) works in percent, where 100% is the recording as played.

To fix the original tempo, tap on the Tempo card and choose Set Original Tempo…. You can type the tempo, tap it in time with the recording, or pick a suggestion such as the piece's own tempo. This is also the way to correct a measurement that came out at half or double the real tempo. Depending on what's shown, the same menu also offers Use Detected Tempo (back to the measured tempo) and Use Percent (no BPM for this recording).

The Key card's works the same way, with Set Original Key…, Use Detected Key and Use Semitones.

Record yourself in the reader

On a piece without a recording, tap Play and choose Record. A recorder card takes the player's place above the score, so you can keep reading and turning pages while you record.

  1. Tap the record button. With 3-2-1 Countdown on, a short count-in comes first; tap during it to cancel.
  2. Play. The card shows the time and a live waveform.
  3. Tap the button again to stop.
  4. Listen back with play, then tap Save to attach the take to the piece, or to record it again.

3-2-1 Countdown is on until you turn it off, and the app remembers your choice. If you leave the piece while holding a take, the app asks Save Recording?. Closing the recorder with asks before discarding a take.

The first time you record, the app asks for access to the microphone.
