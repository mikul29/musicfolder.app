Music Folder keeps your sheet music, chord charts and set lists in one library on iPhone, iPad and Mac, and opens them in a reader you can play from. Here's how the app is laid out, and where to try it out with two example pieces before you add your own music.

## The first time you open the app

A short welcome film plays first. Tap **Get Started** and the app asks **What kind of music do you play?** Choose **Sheet Music & Scores** or **Chord Charts & Songs** to open an example piece with a short guided tour, choose **I play both** to see both, or tap **Skip for now** to go straight to Home. You can come back to the examples at any time (see below).

## Home

{shot:welcome-home}

Home is where the app opens. It shows the pieces you opened recently, your next dated set list (with **Start Set** to play it from the top), today's practice time and goal, and your folders. Tags you've given your pieces are at the bottom.

When your library is empty, Home shows **Import from Files** and **Browse Free Scores** instead, plus **Scan Sheet Music** (on devices with a camera) and **New Chord Chart**. [Add your first music](/guide/add-your-first-music) walks through the first import.

## Library

The Library holds three lists:

- **Music**: every piece in your library, as a list or a grid.
- **Folders**: groups of pieces, such as one folder per band or per concert. A piece can be in more than one folder without making a copy.
- **Set Lists**: pieces in the order you'll play them, ready to play straight through.

On iPhone, tap the **Library** tab, then the list you want. The Library screen also shows your tags and the pieces you played recently.

{shot:welcome-library}

On iPad with iPadOS 18 or later, the Library is in the sidebar. If the sidebar is hidden, show it with the sidebar button at the top left, then tap the list you want under **Library**. Your folders, your upcoming and most recent set lists, and your tags have their own sections further down the sidebar, so you can open one directly. On iPadOS 17, the iPad uses the same tab bar as iPhone.

## Tools

The **Tools** tab has the **Tuner**, **Tone** (reference pitches and drones), **Metronome** and **Piano**. The tuner, metronome and piano are also on the reader's toolbar, so you don't have to leave your music to use them.

## Search

Tap {sf:magnifyingglass} **Search** to open **Browse**. What you type there is looked up in your own library and set lists, and in a catalog of free public-domain sheet music you can add to your library. See [Get free sheet music](/guide/get-free-sheet-music).

## The reader

{shot:welcome-reader}

Tap any piece to open it in the reader. Swipe to turn pages, and use the toolbar for annotation, notes, the metronome and the other tools. Tap **⋯** for more options. [The reader at a glance](/guide/the-reader-at-a-glance) names every part.

## Account

Account holds your practice goal and reminders, iCloud Sync, Backup & Restore, Planning Center, preferences, help and your Premium plan. To open it, tap your picture or initials at the top of Home. Where the app shows a sidebar (iPad and Mac), tap your name, or **Account**, at the bottom of the sidebar instead.

## Try the examples

Two example pieces come with the app: **Welcome to Music Folder**, a chord chart with a recording, and **An einem lichten Morgen** by Clara Schumann, a score with a recording. They stay on your device, aren't part of your library and don't count toward the free limits. Premium tools such as Transpose work in them for free.

1. Open Account.
2. In the **Support** section, tap **Examples**.
3. Tap an example to open it, or tap {sf:ellipsis.circle} beside it and choose **Show Me Around** for the guided tour.

To undo your markings and changes in an example, tap {sf:ellipsis.circle} beside it and choose **Reset Example**.
