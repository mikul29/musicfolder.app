The piano is a full 88-key keyboard, from A0 to C8, that you can play right over the music or on its own. It opens on middle C, which makes it quick for finding a starting pitch or checking a chord in the score.

## Open the piano

- **In a piece on iPhone**, tap {sf:pianokeys} **Piano** in the toolbar (or the **⋯** menu). The keyboard opens in a sheet at the bottom, with the score still visible above it.
- **In a piece on iPad and Mac**, tap {sf:pianokeys} **Piano**; if it isn't on your toolbar, it's in the **⋯** menu. The keyboard is docked inside the reader, so you can still turn pages. Drag the strip along its top edge to move it between the top and the bottom of the page; the app remembers where you left it. Tap **Piano** again to close it.
- **From the Tools tab**, tap **Piano**. It opens straight into the full keyboard.

In a narrow iPad window, the piano uses the iPhone sheet.

Note: Without Premium, tapping **Piano** in a piece or in the Tools tab opens the upgrade screen. See [Free and Premium](/guide/free-and-premium).

## Play and move along the keyboard

Tap keys to play them; you can play chords with several fingers. To reach other octaves, drag the slider above the keys (in the sheet or dock it's between {sf:gearshape} and {sf:xmark}). Sliding a finger across the keys doesn't move the keyboard; in the full keyboard it plays a glissando.

## Hold notes with sustain

**Sustain** works like a sustain pedal.

- In the sheet or dock, tap **Sustain** to latch it on (it shows a lock), and tap again to release it. Touch and hold **Sustain** to sustain only while you're holding it.
- In the full keyboard, slide the **Sustain** switch to the right to lock it on and back to the left to release it. Touch and hold the switch to sustain only while you hold it.

## Change the sound and show note names

Tap {sf:gearshape} and choose a sound: **Grand Piano**, **Electric Piano**, **Strings**, **Organ**, **Brass** or **Clarinet**. You hear a short preview as you choose.

Turn on **Note Names** to label every white key with its note name and octave, not only the Cs.

On iPad and Mac, the full keyboard shows the instruments as cards above the keys instead of behind {sf:gearshape}, with a **Note Names** button beside the slider.

## Use the full keyboard on iPhone

In the piano sheet, tap **Landscape**. The full keyboard turns sideways and fills the screen with larger keys. Tap {sf:chevron.left} **Back** in the top-left corner to leave it.
